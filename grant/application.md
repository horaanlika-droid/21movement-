## Project name

21

## One-line description

A number anyone can paint, and an escrowed exchange of services under it: people
post what they need — a tutor, a handyman, a psychologist — offers arrive, the
work happens, escrow pays. The same board carries city problems and construction
projects, paid by milestone.

## Website / repo

- Website: `https://21.city` (in development)
- Repository: `https://github.com/horaanlika-droid/21movement-`

## The ask, in one paragraph

We are not naming a sum. A round knows its own budget better than we do, and a
figure would be either a guess or a fiction. Instead we fix the **split**: 45% of
whatever arrives goes into quest rewards held in escrow, 15% into consented walls
and the artists who paint them, 15% into the PR campaign and the open kit, 10%
into verification and field operations, 6% into safety, legal and insurance, 5%
into infrastructure, 4% into reserve. A smaller grant buys fewer quests and fewer
walls — not cheaper ones, and never an unpaid person. Code is not in the budget:
the team writes it.

## Problem

Two failures, one missing layer.

1. **Public money can't reach the work, small or large.** A €420 bench, a €950
   bike rack: too small for procurement, too visible to ignore. The city can see
   the problem and cannot pay the person standing next to it. A street rebuild
   moves in the other direction: one large contract, no small doors, a hoarding
   and no public timeline for the people who live behind it.
2. **Private need can't find supply without an intermediary.** A tutor hour, a
   bike repair, an hour with a psychologist. People find each other in group
   chats, pay cash, cancel twice, and nothing is recorded — no price history, no
   reputation that survives the chat, no proof the work happened.
3. **Nothing is verifiable afterwards.** Neither a resident nor a funder can find
   out who was asked, who offered, what was paid, or whether it still exists.

What is missing is not an app. It is a bounded ask, public offers, escrow and a
receipt.

## The mark — why the number does the PR

**21** is the campaign. It is chosen precisely because it is a number, not a
brand:

- You cannot confiscate a number or copyright two digits. Nobody needs our
  permission, and we cannot sell the right to use it.
- It needs no translation: chalk on asphalt in Rotterdam, a marker on a lift door
  in Lisbon, a sticker on a broken bench in Lagos — the same two strokes.
- **Amateur is the format.** Polish would kill it. The mark is supposed to look
  made by a person: drips, uneven edges, a five-second TikTok of the spray. A
  number is also a filter: it says which era you are in — the one where the
  person next to the problem can fix it.
- **It is a meme before it is a brand.** No mascot, no slogan, no translation.
  Ten templates, one six-second TikTok script, a stencil in every caption. The joke is
  the number; the caption is the finished quest.
- **Every mark is a link.** The number points at the task page under it — the ask,
  the offers, the reward in escrow, the receipt. The paint is the advertisement;
  the finished work is the proof.

We publish a free **kit** under MIT: an A4 stencil, a sticker sheet, chalk
templates, a task-ticket template, a six-second vertical video script, and one
line of rules at the top — *ask the owner first*. The kit is printed locally and
is not gated.

The campaign is run by **one large PR agency on retainer**, paid per published
placement and per settled quest — not for impressions. Three waves: 20 creators
per city, then the city permissions that make walls legal, then the groups we
never met. The formats are cheap on purpose: a chalked number, a broken bench, a
before/after, a construction hoarding, a "find the 21" walk. Every settled quest
publishes its own receipt, so the record advertises itself. The only metric we
cannot buy is the mark appearing in groups we have never met; that is the one we
report.

**What we will not do.** We do not pay for illegal paint, we do not instruct
anyone to break a rule, and we do not claim ownership of a number. Consented walls
get artists and fees; everything else is other people's art, at their own risk.

### Where the mark sits

On walls and asphalt, and on infrastructure that is broken: a bench, a pothole, a
lift, an overflowing bin. A chalk 21 next to a broken thing is a report that
anyone can photograph, retake and open. The task page shows how long it has been
waiting, and, when it is fixed, the mark is crossed out and stays in the record.

## The exchange — a demand-first services board

21 is a board, not a catalogue. Nobody browses a list of providers; someone posts
a need.

1. **Ask.** One paragraph, a reward ceiling, a deadline. Two minutes, no CV.
2. **Offers arrive.** Named people with a price, a time and a record. Three offers
   is normal; one is enough.
3. **Choose.** The asker decides. Offers appear in the order they came in — never
   boosted, never ranked by spend.
4. **Do the work.** Online or in the street. The reward is already in escrow, so
   nobody works on a promise.
5. **Confirm.** Both sides confirm; escrow releases; the record stays. 72 hours to
   raise a dispute.

### Anatomy of a quest

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| The ask | Plain language, 400 characters. What, where, when. Not a portal job description. |
| Scope cap | One hour, one session, one visit, one small fix. A big project is split into quests and paid by milestone. |
| Reward | Fixed, funded into escrow **before** the first offer is shown. |
| Deadline | A date and an hour. Not "soon", not "flexible", not "ASAP". |
| Proof | Both sides confirm and attach a note or a photo. Silence is not consent. |
| Who may answer | Anyone with a profile. ID check above €50, category checks where the law asks. |

**Why medium complexity.** Low enough to finish in an afternoon, high enough that
reputation matters: tutoring, translation, bike repair, hairdressing, supervised
psychology, handyman work, CV review, moving a sofa. The unit stays medium; the
project above it can be a street, a school roof or a construction site.

**Why not a gig app.** No dispatch algorithm deciding who eats, no commission
taken from the worker before the work exists, no rating that punishes turning a
shift down. No paid placement, no bought reviews, no cash off-platform.

### The same machinery in public space

A quest for a person and a task for a street are the same object: a bounded ask
with a reward and a proof. Only the payer and the verifier change.

| | Personal quest | Civic task |
|---|---|---|
| Payer | The asker, or a funder buying rewards in bulk | Neighbours, a city budget, a matching pool |
| Verifier | Both sides confirm, then a reviewer on dispute | Resident attestor + city partner + 72 h window |
| Proof | A note or a photo | Signed capture, geofence, before and after |
| Where | Online, or anywhere in the city | Public space only, with owner consent |
| Cap | One session, one visit, one small fix | A per-task cap set by whoever funds it |
| Dispute | Panel: evidence, call, decision | Same panel, plus a staked challenge window |

### Large problems and construction sites

The board has three sizes and one protocol:

| Size | Who publishes | How it is paid |
|---|---|---|
| Quest | A person | One reward in escrow, one proof |
| Civic task | Neighbours, a city budget, a matching pool | Per task, verified in public |
| Project | A municipality, a developer, a housing corporation, a residents' group with a mandate | Open tender, then per milestone |

A project is not a giant quest. It is a project page with a budget, a timeline,
milestones and an open tender. Every milestone has a proof, a named attestor and
its own escrow release; any milestone can be split into quest-sized pieces, so a
local handyman can win a piece of a street rebuild without bidding for the whole
contract. A retention holds for 14 days after acceptance.

Construction sites are where this matters most. A hoarding is the largest legal
wall in a city, and it already has an owner. With written consent the mark goes on
the fence next to a QR to the project page: residents see the timeline, report
what is wrong, and watch each milestone settle. Nothing is painted without
permission.

## Trust, without a boss

- **Funded before offers.** A quest cannot collect offers until the reward is in
  escrow. No maybes, no invoice afterwards.
- **Two-sided confirmation.** The asker confirms the work; the provider confirms
  the handover. Silence is not consent: after 72 hours a reviewer steps in.
- **Dispute, not drama.** A dispute freezes the release and opens a three-step
  panel: evidence, a call, a decision. Both sides stay on the record.
- **Safety is a product rule.** Public first meetings, no cash off-platform, no
  medical claims, ID above €50, a one-tap report that pauses a profile, and
  supervised trainees only in psychology.
- **Escrow is non-custodial.** One contract per quest, no proxy admin above user
  funds. Two audits before mainnet, invariant fuzzing, per-quest caps, 3-of-5
  guardian pause with a 48 h delay.

## Interface, on every device

One responsive web app, installable as a PWA. Same data on five classes of
device — phone, tablet, desktop, street kiosk and the TV in a community centre.

| Device | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Phone (360–430 px) | Ask, offer, capture, confirm. One decision per screen, thumb-reachable. |
| Tablet | Split view: offers and evidence side by side. |
| Desktop (1280 px+) | Review queue, audits, exports. |
| Kiosk | Public screen in a library or station; a QR hands the quest to a phone. |
| TV | Any browser, including the community-centre screen: what the street is asking for, no login. |

Under 150 KB of initial JS, no autoplay video, asks queued offline. Tested on a
€120 Android phone and a five-year-old laptop before every release. The number is
the only logo; there is **no logo**.

## Why Ethereum

- **Neutral settlement.** No company sits between a person's euro and the person
  who did the work. The escrow is the counterparty.
- **A global rail.** USDC on Base reaches a provider without a bank account, in
  seconds; SEPA in EUR is the other option, chosen by the provider.
- **A permanent record.** Who was asked, who offered, what was paid — queryable
  even if 21 disappears. Reputation that survives a chat app.
- **Composability.** Matching pools, retro funding and EAS attestations plug into
  the same registry with no new rails.

**On-chain:** reward, selection, confirmation, payment, reputation.
**Off-chain:** names, addresses, session notes, photos — hashes on-chain, personal
data deletable on request.

## What is different

- Non-custodial by construction: one escrow contract per quest.
- Rules pinned before money arrives; a quest cannot be funded until its payment
  logic is immutable.
- Three sizes, one protocol: a quest, a civic task and a project with milestones.
  A construction site is not forced into a gig-sized box, and a handyman is not
  locked out of a large contract.
- Demand-first: no catalogue, no dispatch algorithm, no placement for sale.
- No token, no presale, no revenue share taken from the person doing the work.
- The campaign is a number nobody owns, and the kit is MIT.

## Budget

**Shares, not sums.** The split is published before the first euro moves.

| Line | Share | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| Quest and project rewards in escrow | 45% | one reward = one finished piece |
| Consented walls, artists, materials | 15% | fees, paint, lifts, consent |
| PR campaign and the kit | 15% | one agency, 20 creators, print, film |
| Verification and field ops | 10% | attestors, reviewers, spot audits |
| Safety, legal, insurance | 6% | contracts, DPIA, liability |
| Ops and infrastructure | 5% | hosting, indexer, RPC |
| Reserve | 4% | the week something breaks |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

If the round is smaller, we cut quests, projects and walls — never the escrow,
never the kit, never the fee paid to a person who did the work. Code is not in this budget:
the team writes it.

## What a round buys

| Shape | Scope |
|---|---|
| If the round is small | One city, one board: 60 quests through escrow, one project page (a street), 3 consented walls with paid artists, kit v1 under MIT, 10 askers recruited by hand. |
| The likely shape | Three cities: 250 quests, 9 consented walls, 2 projects (a street and a construction site), PR wave 1 (one large agency, 20 creators), a staffed verification panel, kiosk pilot. |
| If the round is full | Nine cities: 900 quests, 21 consented walls, 3 large projects paid by milestone, kit v2 in three languages, kiosks in three cities, a public export anyone can audit. |

**The floor.** Below the smallest shape we ship the kit, one city and the escrow
contract — and say publicly that is what happened.

## Milestones

Shares, not sums: each milestone releases a fixed percentage of whatever the grant
turns out to be. No milestone closes on a slide.

| # | Month | Name | Acceptance criterion | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | 1 | Board live + kit v1 | 1 city live: ask, offers, escrow, settlement. Kit v1 published. 10 quests done. | 10% |
| M2 | 2–3 | 3 cities, 150 quests | 150 quests completed, 7 consented walls, first dispute resolved in public. | 30% |
| M3 | 3–4 | Public space + first project | Civic tasks live with attestors; 60 repairs settled; first project page (a street) with milestone escrow; kiosk pilot. | 25% |
| M4 | 4–5 | 9 cities, 900 quests, 3 projects | 900 quests, 21 consented walls, 3 large projects paid by milestone; 100% of walls with written consent. | 25% |
| M5 | 6 | Kit v2 + open export | Everything exported, kit v2 under MIT, exhibition of the 21 walls. | 10% |

An unmet criterion means an unpaid milestone, not a renegotiation.

## The kit, and how it travels

In the kit: an A4 stencil; a sticker sheet of twelve 21s; two chalk templates
(asphalt, lift door); a task-ticket template to photograph next to the mark; a
six-second vertical video script with no speaking; and the rules on the first
line — *ask the owner*.

Three waves: (1) month 1, one agency retainer, 20 local creators, 3 cities;
(2) months 2–4, city permissions make walls legal and every settled quest posts
its own before/after; (3) months 4–6, groups we have never met print the sheet —
the only metric we cannot buy.

## Success metrics (targets, reported at close-out)

- 900 quests completed through escrow (escrow events)
- 7 days median from ask to confirmed work (timestamps)
- 60% of askers post a second quest (repeat asks)
- 21 consented walls painted and registered (consent registry)
- 3 large projects paid by milestone (project registry)
- 0 euros paid outside escrow (reconciliation)
- 5 device classes tested every week (release checklist)

**Guardrails — we fail if these move the wrong way:** 0 off-escrow payments,
disputes under 8% of quests, fraud under 1% of released value, 0 walls without
consent.

Every number is a target for month six, not a result. The methodology ships with
the report.

## Risks

| Risk | Why it is real | Mitigation in the MVP |
|---|---|---|
| The mark gets hijacked | Anyone can paint 21, including people we would not choose | We never claim ownership. The task page is the only canonical link; the kit states the rules on its first line |
| Illegal paint in our name | Stencils travel; walls have owners | We never pay or instruct. Consented walls get artists and fees; the kit says ask first, in three languages |
| A cold, empty board | An empty board attracts nobody and the number stops meaning anything | Seed three paid civic tasks per city and 20 askers recruited by curators, before any PR spend |
| Two strangers meet | In-person quests carry real physical risk | Public first meetings, ID above €50, panic button, no cash off-platform, one-tap report that pauses a profile |
| Bad work, bad practice | An unsupervised therapist, a repair that fails in a week | Category rules, supervised trainees only, a 14-day workmanship window, delisting after two upheld disputes |
| Escrow bug | Contracts hold real money from day one | Two audits, invariant fuzzing, per-quest caps, 3-of-5 guardian pause with 48 h delay |
| Resident privacy | Photos and notes identify people | On-device blur, coarse public coordinates, off-chain personal data, DPIA before pilot 1 |

Open question we bring to reviewers: what is the smallest proof that a stranger
did the work — and how few clicks should it cost?

## Sustainability

- Optional 1.5% fee on released escrow, capped and opt-in per city — never on the
  reward a person earned.
- Hosted instance and support subscription for municipalities.
- Matching pools funded by partners, not by the protocol.
- Year 2: registry ownership renounced; contracts, indexer, client, kit and the
  artist kit stay MIT.

## Короткая версия (RU)

**21** — это число, которое может нарисовать кто угодно: мелом на асфальте,
маркером на двери лифта, стикером на сломанной скамейке. Оно не требует
перевода, разрешения и логотипа, его нельзя запретить или запатентовать. Именно
поэтому оно работает как PR: любительски, быстро, тиктоками, на стенах и на
сломанных местах инфраструктуры. Оно задаёт вектор и чувство эпохи, а не
изображает бренд. 21 должен стать мемом, а не рекламой: крупное PR-агентство на
ретейнере, оплата за опубликованное размещение и за доведённый квест, а не за
показы; десять шаблонов, шесть секунд, трафарет в каждом описании. Мы платим за
согласованные стены, художников, съёмку и бесплатный набор трафаретов (MIT); за
незаконную покраску не платим и никого к ней не призываем.

Под числом — **биржа услуг по спросу**, а не каталог. Человек пишет, что ему
нужен репетитор, психолог, мастер, помощник: указывает награду и срок. Приходят
отклики от конкретных людей с ценой и временем. Автор выбирает, работа
выполняется, обе стороны подтверждают — эскроу выплачивает награду, запись
остаётся публичной. Квест — средняя сложность: час, сессия, визит, мелкий
ремонт. Крупные городские проблемы и стройки публикуются как проекты: бюджет,
этапы, открытый тендер; каждый этап оплачивается по факту и может быть разбит на
квесты, чтобы местный мастер мог взять кусок работ без тендера на весь объект.
Забор стройки с письменного согласия становится самой большой легальной стеной в
городе — с QR на страницу проекта. Без алгоритма раздачи заказов, без платных
мест в выдаче, без комиссии с исполнителя до того, как работа появилась.

Интерфейс — одна адаптивная веб-версия, а не отдельные приложения: телефон,
планшет, десктоп, уличный киоск и телевизор в общественном центре.

**Почему Ethereum:** эскроу вместо посредника, USDC на Base доходит без
банковского счёта, а обещания и выплаты остаются проверяемыми после закрытия
гранта. Без токена, без кастодиального хранения, лицензия MIT.

**Сумму гранта мы не указываем** — раунд сам знает свой бюджет. Вместо суммы
фиксируем пропорции: 45% в награды в эскроу, 15% на согласованные стены и
художников, 15% на PR-кампанию и набор, 10% на верификацию и полевую работу, 6%
на безопасность и страховку, 5% на инфраструктуру, 4% в резерв. Меньше грант —
меньше квестов и стен, но не дешевле и никогда за счёт людей, которые сделали
работу.
